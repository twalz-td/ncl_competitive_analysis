Section 01
Booking health — actualized vs. cancelled

Of 33,504 booking records from 19,283 unique customers, only 19,966 (60%) represent trips that were actually taken. 12,635 bookings were cancelled, representing 5,558 customers who never travelled with Exodus. The realised revenue base is £70.6M — not the £125M+ that appears when cancelled bookings are included. Everything that follows in this analysis is grounded in the 13,725 customers who actually travelled.

COVID years excluded from comparative analysis. Departures in 2020 and 2021 had a 78.3% cancellation rate — an extraordinary outlier driven by global travel shutdowns, not business patterns. Headline totals below include all years. All comparative charts and insights exclude 2020–2021 departure dates.

What this shows

Cancellation rates from 2017 onward tell a clear story: 7% in 2017, rising to 25% in 2018, then a significant post-COVID spike to 42–52% in 2022–2024. The 52% cancellation rate on 2024 departures stands out as an area of focus — one in two bookings for that departure year did not result in a completed trip.

Seed audience recommendation

The seed is built entirely from the 13,725 customers who actually travelled with Exodus — no cancelled bookings, no unconverted prospects. Two gates: composite value scoring on behavioral signals, then Mosaic alignment filtering. The logic drove the size.

How the seed was built — audience cascade

.

Recommended seed: 11,666 unique actualized customers

Two sequential gates applied to the 13,725 customers who actually travelled: composite value scoring (bottom 15% removed) then Mosaic alignment filtering (non-classifiable and non-aligned groups removed). All scoring done at the customer level — each customer represented by their full travel history with Exodus, not a single booking.

Score every actualized customer on composite value — gate one

All 13,725 customers who actually travelled are scored on three behavioral signals: lifetime revenue (50%), actualized trip count (30%), and median booking window (20%). Scoring at the customer level means a repeater with 5 trips is evaluated on their full travel relationship with Exodus. The bottom 15% — 2,059 customers averaging £1,081 lifetime revenue, 1.0 trips, and a 72-day booking window — are removed as weak signal. These are customers who travelled once, spent minimally, and booked close to departure. 11,666 customers advance to gate two.

2

No Mosaic exclusion — Experian closes the data gap

Of the 11,666 customers in the seed, 9,094 have a Mosaic code and 2,572 do not. All 11,666 are included. The 2,572 without a current code are actualized, high-value Exodus customers — they passed the same behavioral value gate as everyone else. Experian will assign Mosaic codes during the matching process, so excluding them would penalise good customers for a data gap the modeling process will resolve.

3

The result — 11,666 actualized, high-signal customers

The seed consists entirely of customers who planned their trip well, spent meaningfully, and followed through. 2,880 repeaters (25%), 8,786 one-time customers (75%). Average lifetime revenue £5,860 — 14% above the full actualized-customer average of £5,143. Median lifetime revenue £4,112. The seed is grounded in actual travel behavior — not booking intent — which is the strongest possible foundation for a lookalike model.
